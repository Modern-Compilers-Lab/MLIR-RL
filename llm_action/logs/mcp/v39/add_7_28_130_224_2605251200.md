**Key observations:**
- **Vec and ParVec SUCCEED on linalg.add** (named op with simple identity indexing maps, unlike conv/pooling which have strided or windowed access patterns).
- ParallelVectorization[1,4,1,16] is the best single action: **1.40ms (5.64x, beats PyTorch at 1.57x!)**
- TilingBasedParallelization and DirectParallelization also beat PyTorch (~1.2x).
- DirPar[16] with only 7 elements in dim0 → only 7 of 16 threads do work, limiting parallelism.
- TilPar[0,4,0,0] splits dim1=28 into 7 chunks → 7 parallel tasks.
- Vectorization alone barely helps (8.46ms vs 7.89ms base) due to loop overhead from step-1 inner loops on non-divisible dims.
- Tiling alone hurts performance (12.94ms) due to overhead without vectorization benefit.
- Im2colLowering **FAILS precondition** (requires conv_2d_nchw_fchw).
- Vector sizes [1,4,1,16]: d0=7 (not div by 2/4/8/16 → 1), d1=28 (div by 4), d2=130 (not div by 2/4/8/16 → 1), d3=224 (div by 16).

**Key finding:** Standalone ParallelVectorization remains the best. Multi-step DirPar → ParVec creates nested forall constructs which adds overhead without benefit. The single ParVec already parallelizes across all 4 dimensions (7×7×130×14 = 89,180 parallel tasks), fully utilizing available cores.

## Phase 4: Local Tuning Summary

Best schedule: **ParallelVectorization[1,4,1,16]** at **1.40ms** (5.64x vs MLIR base, **1.57x vs PyTorch**)

This kernel **beats PyTorch** with ParVec, TilPar, and DirPar. Vector sizes [1,4,1,16] are optimal because dims 7 and 130 are not divisible by any available vector size > 1.
