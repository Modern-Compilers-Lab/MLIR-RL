### Key Observations

1. **Universal Best Schedule**: `TBP([4,0,0]) -> vectorization([4,8,8])` works best across all 4 kernels, providing 45-240x speedup over naive MLIR.

2. **Parallelization is the Dominant Factor**: Both TBP and TCP provide the largest single-action speedups (14-27x). The 28-core Broadwell benefits enormously from parallelization.

3. **Vectorization Complements Parallelization**: Vectorization provides 8-15x as a single action. When combined with parallelization, the benefits multiply.

4. **Post-Vectorization Composability is Limited**: After vectorization, almost no action can be applied (postconditions fail). This makes vectorization a natural "terminal" action in schedules.

5. **Small K Benefits Parallelization More**: Kernel 1 (K=128) and Kernel 2 (K=256) achieve higher relative speedup because the parallel slices have less reduction work per thread.

6. **Gap to PyTorch**: The MLIR schedule reaches 32-74% of PyTorch (MKL) performance. The gap is larger for bigger K dimensions where MKL's highly-optimized BLAS routines excel.

7. **Packing and Promotion**: Both provide modest speedups (2-5x) but are outclassed by parallelization + vectorization. Promotion converts to memref semantics which limits subsequent transformations.

8. **Loop Interchange**: Permutation [0,2,1] (moving K loop inward) helps for large N (6.85x on K1), while [1,0,2] (swapping M and N loops) hurts performance.

9. **Split Reduction**: Minimal benefit for K=128. Factor of 4 gives 3.34x for K1 but still far below parallelization approaches.

10. **Tiling alone**: Tile sizes of [32,32,32] give best single-tiling results (6.08x on K1), consistent with cache-line optimization for Broadwell's 256KB L1 data cache per core.
