## What is compared

Only the **un-optimized baselines** over the **intersection** of benchmark names.
- **MLIR baseline**: old `MLIR` column vs new `mlir` field.
- **Torch baseline**: old `PyTorch JIT` column vs new `torch` field (both JIT-compiled).
- Units normalized to **milliseconds** (old CSV is in ns → ÷1e6; new JSON already ms).
- Match scope: new **`eval`** split only. `dataset_conv2d_img2col` excluded.
- **Ratio = new / old** per benchmark. **GMR** = geometric-mean ratio (GMR < 1 ⇒ new environment is *faster*; > 1 ⇒ *slower*).

## Key insights

### MLIR baseline — effectively environment-independent

- **MLIR baseline (overall)** (n=62): the new environment is **1.03× slower** on average (≈ no change) (GMR=1.025, 95% CI [0.982, 1.070], Wilcoxon p=8.9e-04 — statistically significant). New faster on 14/62, slower on 48/62; 97% agree within 2×.
- Per-category GMRs all sit near 1.0 with near-perfect rank/log correlation (Spearman ≈ 1.0, Pearson(log) ≈ 1.0). The handful of >1.5× rows are tiny tensors (e.g. `add_256_7_7_176`, `add_256_14_14_88`) where fixed measurement overhead dominates. **The compiled MLIR runtime is the same across systems → MLIR baselines (and MLIR-relative speedups) are directly comparable.**

### Torch baseline (PyTorch JIT) — strongly op-dependent and bidirectional

- **Torch baseline (overall)** (n=62): the new environment is **1.47× faster** on average (GMR=0.678, 95% CI [0.521, 0.882], Wilcoxon p=0.054 — not significant). New faster on 30/62, slower on 32/62; 52% agree within 2×. The discrepancy shrinks with op size (slope=-0.188, r=-0.47).
- The overall GMR is misleading because the per-category shifts point in opposite directions and partly cancel (median ratio ≈ 1.0, yet ~half the rows differ by >2×). Broken down, the new Torch baseline is **faster** in the new env for `matmul` (4.59×), `pooling` (6.16×); **slower** for `add` (1.24×), `relu` (1.67×); **unchanged** for `conv2d`.
- The biggest divergence is small-shape matmul: old `PyTorch JIT` times for small matmuls cluster around a ~4 ms floor (e.g. `matmul_256_256_512` = 4.09 ms) regardless of size, while the new env measures 0.2–0.6 ms — i.e. the **old Torch measurement carried a large fixed overhead/floor** that the new warm-up + median-of-5 methodology removes. This makes new matmul/pooling Torch baselines much faster.
- **Consequence:** Torch-relative speedups are NOT comparable across the two systems. A matmul that looked competitive vs. old Torch will look ~4–5× worse vs. new Torch; pooling ~6× worse; conversely add/relu targets are easier in the new env.

Per-benchmark values are in [`baseline_comparison.csv`](baseline_comparison.csv). Wilcoxon p-values use a normal approximation (scipy unavailable); per-category n is small, so treat per-category significance cautiously and rely on the overall scope.
